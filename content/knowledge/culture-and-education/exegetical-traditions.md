Exegesis is the work of drawing meaning out of a text held to be authoritative. It becomes a *tradition* when that work is codified: explicit rules of reading, a chain of transmission, and a community that validates or rejects interpretations. This is not free reading. It is a craft with constraints, and those constraints are the interesting part.

## The Major Traditions

Each of the great exegetical lineages developed a layered model of meaning, where the same text yields different senses at different depths of reading. [[hermeneutics|Hermeneutics]] treats several of these lineages in detail; the map below is compressed to expose what they share.

**Judaism.** The PaRDeS model distinguishes four levels: *peshat* (plain), *remez* (allusive), *derash* (homiletic), *sod* (secret). It structures both Midrashic interpretation and Kabbalistic reading.

**Christianity.** The four senses of Scripture: literal, allegorical, moral, anagogical. Formalised by Origen and systematised in medieval scholasticism.

**Islam.** The distinction between *zahir* (apparent) and *batin* (hidden), with *tafsir* addressing the first and *ta'wil* the second. Developed most extensively in Shia and Sufi traditions.

**India.** The Vedantic commentary schools, where the *bhashya* is a genre in its own right and where one inherits from a teacher not just doctrine but a manner of reading.

**China.** Successive layers of commentary on the Confucian classics, which themselves become objects of commentary, producing a stratified corpus rather than a fixed text.

## The Shared Structure

Underneath the differences, three features recur:

1. **Stratification.** Meaning is layered, and the surface reading is valid but not exhaustive. The deeper senses do not cancel the literal one.

2. **A validating community.** An interpretation is not correct because it feels right to the reader. It is admissible because a community that shares the rules accepts it. Validity is intersubjective, not private.

3. **Lineage.** Reading competence transmits through relationship. You learn to read from someone who learned to read. It moves the way any craft moves, through apprenticeship rather than through manuals: see the [[guild-model|Guild Model]].

## Why This Matters Beyond Religion

Modern hermeneutics comes directly out of this. Schleiermacher's founding gesture is to generalise the rules of biblical exegesis to *any* text, sacred or not. Dilthey widens the scope again, to every expression of life. Ricoeur extends it to action and to history, treating them as readable in the manner of a text.

Philosophical hermeneutics is therefore, quite literally, secularised exegesis. This explains something that is otherwise puzzling: why training in these traditions produces reading reflexes that remain operative far outside their original domain. What was learned was never really about the specific text. It was about how to hold multiple levels of meaning at once without collapsing them into one.

The classical [[trivium|Trivium]] is the secular cousin of this training. Grammar, logic, and rhetoric are the disciplines that make a text tractable before any question of depth arises, which is why they were taught first and why the layered readings were built on top of them rather than instead of them.

## A Note on Validation

There is a structural echo worth following. An exegetical tradition is a set of rules determining what counts as an admissible reading within a community, plus a mechanism for applying them. [[agent-centric-architecture|Agent-centric distributed architectures]] encode something formally similar: [[validation-rules|validation rules]] that determine what counts as an admissible entry within a network, applied by peers rather than by an authority. In both cases, validity is produced by a shared membrane rather than by correspondence to a fact.

The difference is that one membrane is inherited and the other is written. That difference is a design decision, and it is worth making consciously. [[validation-rules-as-interpretive-membrane|Validation Rules as Interpretive Membrane]] follows the echo through to where it breaks.
